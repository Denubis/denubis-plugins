# Focused study-design checks

Use these questions where the manuscript's design makes them relevant. They are
prompts for finding evidence, not presumptions of defects or a scoring sheet.

- **Definitions and criteria:** Is the claimed property defined independently of
  the outcome used to demonstrate it? Does a measure include part of the criterion
  it is later said to predict? Identify the overlap and its consequence.
- **Manipulation and exposure:** What did participants or systems actually receive?
  Compare the intended intervention with the delivered materials, prompts, timing,
  instructions, and checks. A condition label does not establish the contrast.
- **Selection and exclusions:** Were inclusion decisions made before exposure or
  using variables affected by it? Trace denominators and exclusions by condition;
  assess which population and estimand the remaining observations can support.
- **Independence and generalisation:** Are repeated observations from the same
  participant, document, model, or site treated as independent evidence? Separate
  the unit measured, assigned, analysed, and generalised to.
- **Absence and equivalence:** Does an inconclusive difference become a claim of
  equality? Look for an explicit equivalence margin and an analysis capable of
  supporting that claim; report uncertainty at the strength the design permits.
- **Mechanism and alternatives:** Could measurement, leakage, instructions, order,
  or another change explain the result? Name a concrete competing explanation and
  the observation that would discriminate it, rather than demanding every control.

For each concern, locate the actual method and result, search supplements for a
recovery, and distinguish a design limitation from missing reporting. Do not infer
that an unreported safeguard was absent. Apply the main skill's evidence and
promotion rules before returning a finding.
