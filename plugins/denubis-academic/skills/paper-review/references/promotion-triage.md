# Promote review evidence into author-facing findings

A review observation is not automatically an author-facing problem. Promote it only when
current text and inspected evidence establish a defect, uncertainty, or decision with a
concrete consequence.

For each candidate:

1. Re-open the exact current anchor and follow relevant cross-references through the whole
   in-scope manuscript.
2. State the minimum defect the text actually supports. An unfamiliar choice, trigger, or
   possible alternative is not yet a defect.
3. Name the consequence for validity, evidence, transparency, source fidelity, argument,
   self-sufficiency, register, or materially costly flow. “Could be clearer” is not enough.
4. Search for the strongest on-page definition, qualification, distinction, rebuttal, or
   competing reading that could resolve or narrow the concern. State the search scope.
5. Distinguish evidence gaps, author decisions, editorial actions, and reviewer limits.
6. Promote, retain as background evidence, merge, batch, defer, narrow, or discard the
   candidate with a short reason.

Genre locality matters: an abstract, definition, table, or other stand-alone surface may
still fail even if a later section eventually supplies the missing boundary. Conversely,
do not keep a stronger diagnosis when the manuscript already supplies enough recovery.

Merge findings that require the same author decision or repair the same root defect.
Batch local faults that need no separate author judgment. Keep a request for evidence
separate from a prose preference. Promote a cut only when it removes a complete unused
branch, demonstrated repetition, or another concrete burden without discarding a claim,
warrant, qualification, or necessary orientation.

After consolidation, deliberately recheck each survivor:

- Does current text support the diagnosis and consequence?
- Does another passage resolve it at the required locality?
- Is the claim merely arguable, speculative, or based on an invited implication?
- Does another survivor require the same response?
- Is optional polish masquerading as a substantive problem?

There is no target finding count, rejection rate, report length, or fixed number of model
review loops. Do not optimize the report to its own rubric.

Every promoted finding contains its exact anchor and evidence, minimum diagnosis, concrete
consequence, strongest competing reading, observation that would change the assessment,
and required response class. Ask one pointed question only when authorial intent or
authority is genuinely required.
