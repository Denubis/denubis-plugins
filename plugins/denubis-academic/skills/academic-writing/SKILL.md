# Academic Writing

## Load the project's register

Before the first prose edit, list every Markdown file under the project-owned .notes/
directory, including hidden and ignored paths. Read every note's frontmatter, then open the
full body of notes whose description or type concerns prose, register, citation, venue,
voice, or formatting. Project instructions and an attested register override this portable
method.

If no register exists, do not invent a durable authorial voice. Use clear academic prose
for the current task. When future work would benefit from a register, derive a draft from
an author-approved corpus, state its evidence and limits, agree its wording with the
author, then write a project note. The bundled register template is a starting structure,
not a phrase or filename detector.

## Preserve meaning before changing prose

For the passage being revised, identify:

- the substantive claim or function of each sentence;
- the evidence, scope, qualifier, and attribution that calibrate it;
- the reader and venue requirements;
- citations, quotations, terminology, numbers, and cross-references that must remain
  exact; and
- the reviewer or author request the change must satisfy.

Do not silently add a claim, strengthen a hedge, change a statistical or methodological
meaning, or use fluent wording to hide an unresolved source problem.

## Remove scar tissue

Scar tissue talks about managing the manuscript instead of carrying its substance:
unnecessary foreshadowing, provenance housekeeping, defensive replies to off-page
reviewers, journal-policy justification, and structural announcements with no content.
Replace it with the claim, connection, design fact, or limitation in the place where that
substance belongs.

Not every metadiscursive sentence is scar. A venue may require a contribution statement,
a methods sentence may describe an actual procedure, and an epistemic hedge may be
load-bearing. The project register and the sentence's evidential function decide.

## Rebuild sentences from the idea

An overloaded sentence normally contains several independent claims, parenthetical
defences, or punctuation doing structural work. Recover the primary idea and rebuild its
relations with ordinary syntax. Do not merely swap one mark for another, and do not
over-correct into a stack of short disconnected sentences.

Use plain domain language before jargon, define acronyms at first use, and give statistics
the interpretation the intended reader needs. Punctuation, first person, connective
density, spelling, and citation presentation follow the attested project or venue
register—not a universal model preference.

For APA-style journal paraphrases, page locators are normally optional; direct quotations
need a locator. Books, reproduced numerical values, and project-specific verification
rules may justify one. Source fidelity and the venue's citation style remain separate
questions.

## Verify the revision

Read the new passage against the original and its sources. Check that every substantive
claim, qualification, citation relationship, number, defined term, and required response
survives at the intended strength. Read it aloud for cadence and paragraph flow. Search
the bounded current manuscript for changed facts or terms and inspect each real occurrence;
do not treat an empty search or a banned-phrase count as prose quality.

Leave dated historical records unchanged. A working note or prior model draft is not
verified source material; recover facts from the primary source before promoting them.
