## Read this first

- **Assessment levels.** Every row was assessed from its title, its abstract, or (for the one
  non-scholarly web page) the page text. No paper was read in full. The dispatch excluded Zotero
  and PDF downloads, so the skill's reading gate was never opened. Abstract-level relevance is
  not a source-fidelity check. Any claim about methods or results beyond the abstract needs
  full-text reading through `using-bibliography`.
- **Directions.** "LLM > human" or "LLM < human" is recorded only where the abstract states the
  direction. "Not stated" means the abstract names the feature but gives no direction.
- **Topic codes.** T1 measured grammatical or rhetorical features. T2 decay of lexical markers
  across models and prompting. T3 human perception and blind testing. T4 reusable tooling, code
  or data. T5 academic manuscripts, peer review, or journal and publisher guidance.
- **Model coverage.** Few abstracts name models released from mid-2025 onward. Where a model
  name appeared only in a search-engine snippet, the row says it is unverified.
- **Identifiers** are copied as the registry returned them. No citekeys exist for these items
  because Zotero was out of scope, and none were made up.
- **Citation counts** (Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex, retrieved 2026-10-17) are in the saved
  responses. Ranking does not use them.

Ranking criteria, in order: the paper measures structural rather than purely lexical features;
it covers models from mid-2025 or later, or tracks change over model generations; the number of
wanted topics it serves; and whether it studies academic or peer-review text. The ranking is my
judgement, not a quality score.
